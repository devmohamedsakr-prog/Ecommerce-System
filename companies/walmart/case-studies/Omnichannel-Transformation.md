# Walmart Case Study: Omnichannel Transformation

## Starting Position (2010)
- Physical retail: $400B+ (primary business)
- Ecommerce: $2B (negligible)
- Gap: Amazon growing 30%+ YoY
- Challenge: Walmart late to ecommerce

## Investment Phase (2010-2018)
- Tech hiring: 1000s of engineers
- Infrastructure: Build ecommerce platform
- Acquisition: Jet.com ($3.3B, 2016)
- Marketplace: Enable third-party sellers
- Supply chain: Modernization

## Omnichannel Breakthrough (2018-2023)
- BOPIS launch: 2018 (pilot)
- Rapid expansion: 2019-2020 (during COVID)
- Ship-from-store: Scaled aggressively
- Store network: Became competitive advantage
- Result: Ecommerce growth 30%+ YoY

## Financial Results
- 2020: $50B ecommerce (25% growth during COVID)
- 2023: $100B ecommerce (20% growth)
- 2026: $130B ecommerce (forecast)
- Physical retail: Flat-to-declining ($320B)
- Total GMV: $450B+ (vs Amazon $600B+)

## Competitive Positioning
- Amazon: Leader (2x Walmart ecommerce)
- Walmart: #2 (faster than expected)
- Gap closing: Omnichannel advantage
- Geographic: Walmart closer to customers

## Key Victories
1. **BOPIS Market Share:** 30% of orders
   - vs Amazon 5% (less relevant, no stores)
   - vs Target 20% (fewer stores than Walmart)

2. **Same-Day Delivery:** Available 2000+ areas
   - via BOPIS + ship-from-store
   - Competes with Amazon Prime

3. **Marketplace Growth:** 50M+ SKUs
   - Third-party seller commission
   - 20% of GMV from marketplace

4. **Profitability:** Ecommerce approaching break-even
   - BOPIS: Lower shipping cost
   - Ship-from-store: Inventory turns
   - Result: Margin improving
